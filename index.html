Emily Martin

PhD Candidate, Biological Sciences · University of Idaho
NSF Graduate Research Fellow

I study snakes at two scales. Evolutionarily, I ask why clutch size varies so much among snake species and across geography. Practically, I ask what happens to rattlesnakes when people move them out of harm's way, and why some do not survive.

My fieldwork centers on prairie rattlesnakes (Crotalus viridis) in Wyoming, where I track translocated snakes with surgically implanted radio transmitters. I work with state and federal wildlife agencies to turn the results into practical guidance for people who share the landscape with rattlesnakes.
