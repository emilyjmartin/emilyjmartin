Emily Martin

Evolutionary ecologist studying trait evolution and pit viper translocation

I use phylogenetic comparative methods, bioacoustics, and movement modeling to answer questions about life history evolution and to address conflict between humans and wildlife. My current research interests include the mechanisms that underlie clutch size variation, the evolution of tree frog advertising calls, and the outcomes of post-translocation mortality in pit vipers.

Research themes

Three questions run through my work.

01

Life history evolution

Phylogenetic comparative analyses of clutch size variation, both across snake species and within rattlesnakes across their range.

02

Signal evolution

Bioacoustic and comparative approaches to the mechanisms behind variation in tree frog advertising calls.

03

Human–wildlife conflict

Movement modeling and field research on the outcomes of pit viper translocation, including post-translocation mortality.
