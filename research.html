Projects

My dissertation, Evolution of Clutch Size and the Causes of Translocation Mortality in Snakes, is advised by Courtney Conway and Luke Harmon and supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. Alongside it, I study snake natural history and the evolution of frog calls.

Published 2026Ecology & Evolution

Shedding Aggregations in Prairie Rattlesnakes

We made a novel observation of prairie rattlesnake (Crotalus viridis) aggregations during ecdysis, the process of shedding skin, and tested competing hypotheses to explain why snakes cluster during this vulnerable period.

Read the paper Martin & Conway (2026), Ecology and Evolution 16(4): e73311.

AcceptedHerpetological Review

Rattle Loss in Prairie Rattlesnakes

We documented skeletal and reproductive abnormalities in prairie rattlesnakes (Crotalus viridis) with rattle loss.

Martin & Conway (2026), Crotalus viridis (Prairie Rattlesnake): morphology. Herpetological Review, accepted for publication.

In preparationBioacoustics

Evolution of Tree Frog Advertising Calls

We used phylogenetic comparative methods and bioacoustic techniques to test hypotheses explaining the mechanisms of call variation in Rhacophorid tree frogs.

Co-authors: S. Duba, T. Hayes, L. J. Harmon, and K. Corn. “Explaining Variation in Acoustic Signals of Tree Frogs,” in preparation.
