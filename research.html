Research

My dissertation connects evolutionary questions about snake reproduction with applied questions about snake conservation and human–wildlife conflict. Funded by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship.

Dissertation Chapter

Evolution of Clutch Size in Snakes

Clutch size varies enormously among snakes, from a single egg to dozens of offspring, and also varies geographically within groups of related species. Using a phylogenetic comparative approach, this project examines how ecology, body size, climate, and geography relate to clutch-size variation across snakes, and what these patterns reveal about the evolution of reproductive strategies. Advisor: Luke Harmon.

Dissertation Chapter

Causes of Translocation Mortality in Prairie Rattlesnakes

Moving venomous snakes away from homes and campgrounds is a popular but controversial management tool. This project follows prairie rattlesnakes (Crotalus viridis) at Sinks Canyon State Park, Wyoming, using surgically implanted VHF transmitters to compare survival of translocated and resident snakes and to identify what drives mortality after release. The goal is evidence-based guidance for managers deciding whether, and how, to relocate rattlesnakes. Advisor: Courtney Conway.

Manuscript in preparation Presented at the Idaho Chapter of The Wildlife Society (2025) and the Wyoming Game and Fish All-District Meeting (2025).

Project

Shedding Aggregations in Prairie Rattlesnakes

Prairie rattlesnakes are often found gathered together while preparing to shed their skin. This project evaluates potential causes of these shedding aggregations, considering ecological and behavioral explanations for why snakes cluster during this vulnerable period.

In review: Ecology & Evolution

Natural History Note

Morphology of the Prairie Rattlesnake

A short natural history note documenting morphological measurements of Crotalus viridis in the northern part of its range, contributing baseline data for a widely distributed but under-measured species.

In review: Herpetological Review
